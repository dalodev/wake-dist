License ​

Two licenses cover Wake, a third its documentation, and a fourth its older releases.

The server and the web app ​

  • offering it to others as a hosted or managed service;
  • removing or obscuring its licensing notices;
  • getting around its license-key functionality or the free plan's limits, such as the number of people, or removing what enforces them.

Letting the people the free plan allows use an installation you run is not offering it as a hosted or managed service.

The terms grant no right to use the Wake name or logo, except to say truthfully that your work uses or is based on Wake. The laws of Spain govern them, and the courts of the licensor's domicile in Spain decide a dispute, unless the law gives you other courts, such as those where you live as a consumer. You run Wake on your own machines and keep your data and its backups; the terms limit liability only as far as the law allows.

The free plan ​

Under the free plan, at most 3 people in all may use Wake for you and your company, however many installations you run, each with their own account, unless a paid license allows more. An account is never shared between people. Everyone who uses Wake through a bot, an integration or a shared tool counts as a person; agents and API clients never count themselves. An installation is one running copy of the server with its database, on however many machines or containers.

Each installation holds its part of the limit itself, 3 accounts: see Free plan accounts.

Read the terms ​

Each release carries its terms as LICENSE:

  • The installer writes them into the installation's directory as LICENSE, and its checklist says ✓ License: Wake Free License. Each upgrade replaces the file with the new release's.
  • Each release lists LICENSE among its files, covered by its signed checksums; see Verify a download.
  • Each image carries it at /usr/share/licenses/wake/LICENSE; see The images.
  • The web app names the license in About this Wake.
